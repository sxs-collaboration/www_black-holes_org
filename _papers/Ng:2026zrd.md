abstract: |
  Binary neutron-star (BNS) mergers are multimessenger sources whose
  kilonova emission and nucleosynthesis depend sensitively on neutrino
  microphysics and flavor conversion. Most BNS merger simulations
  assume \(μ\)-\(τ\) neutrino degeneracy in a two-flavor treatment. We
  investigate the fast flavor instability (FFI) and the collisional
  flavor instability (CFI) in long-lived BNS merger remnants using
  axisymmetric general-relativistic three-flavor Boltzmann neutrino
  transport with a comprehensive treatment of muons and muonic
  interactions, following a handoff from 3D grey moment-based
  simulations. Our simulations largely reproduce the parent 3D
  simulations, recovering trapped weak equilibrium in the core, muonic
  non-equilibrium in the semitransparent outer disk due to prolonged
  demuonization, and cold weak equilibrium in transparent regions.
  Linear stability analysis reveals strong and widespread CFI in all
  three flavor sectors, including narrow resonance-like structures.
  Muonic interactions reshape the FFI distribution, shifting the
  \(eτ\)-sector instability toward denser disk regions compared with
  the extended FFI outside the disk in the two-flavor model, while
  disk demuonization produces a strong \(ν_μ\) excess and one-sided
  muon-lepton-number angular distributions that suppress FFI in the
  \(eμ\) and \(μτ\) sectors. Local three-flavor quantum kinetic
  equation (QKE) calculations reveal nonlinear coupling among flavor
  sectors that excites coherence even in an initially stable sector.
  Diagonal collisional repopulation is decisive for the nonlinear
  evolution and asymptotic flavor populations, and neglecting it
  overestimates flavor equilibration. We establish the necessity of
  three-flavor transport from both classical and quantum perspectives,
  and underscore the importance of consistently coupling flavor
  conversion with diagonal collisional repopulation in future CFI
  subgrid models.
