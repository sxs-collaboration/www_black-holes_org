abstract: |
  Gravitational self-force calculations are needed to accurately
  describe the motion of a small body in a spinning (Kerr) black hole
  spacetime, which is vital to predict gravitational wave signals from
  asymmetric-mass compact binaries. Accurate calculations for the
  upcoming LISA mission require a minimum of second perturbative order
  in the mass ratio, which has thus far only been achieved for
  nonspinning (Schwarzschild) black holes due to nonlinear
  complexities of the Einstein equations. In this work, we calculate
  Kerr metric perturbations with a new computational approach based on
  numerical relativity methods, extending recent scalar self-force
  results to full general relativity. We formulate the perturbative
  Lorenz-gauge Einstein equations in terms of azimuthal \(m\)-modes in
  the frequency domain and solve the resulting elliptic partial
  differential equations using a spectral discontinuous Galerkin
  discretization, adaptive mesh refinement, and preconditioned
  iterative solvers. With our new method, we achieve exponentially
  convergent first-order gravitational self-force calculations for
  circular equatorial orbits in Kerr spacetime up to spin
  \(|a|/M=0.998\) (the Thorne limit). Our framework is designed to
  carry over to second order by delivering accurate and well-behaved
  first-order data for nonlinear source construction, and as a solver
  for the second-order metric perturbations themselves.
