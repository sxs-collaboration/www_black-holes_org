abstract: |
  Accretion onto supermassive black holes (BHs) can launch
  relativistic outflows and jets that inject energy and momentum into
  their surroundings. Understanding how such feedback shapes large-
  scale accretion is key to bridging observations from galactic scales
  (e.g., the Bondi radius, <math display="inline"><msub><mi>r</mi><mi
  mathvariant="normal">B</mi></msub></math>) down to event horizon
  scales (<math display="inline"><msub><mi>r</mi><mi
  mathvariant="normal">g</mi></msub></math>), spanning five to six
  orders of magnitude. To address this challenge directly, we treat
  the spatial scale separation as a free parameter, varying it across
  two to four orders of magnitude. We perform a suite of the longest
  contiguous 3D general relativistic magnetohydrodynamic simulations
  to date (<math
  display="inline"><mrow><mi>t</mi><mo>≤</mo><mn>4</mn><mo>×</mo><msup><mrow><mn>10</mn></mrow><mrow><mn>6</mn></mrow></msup><msub><mrow><mi>r</mi></mrow><mrow><mi
  mathvariant="normal">g</mi></mrow></msub><mo>/</mo><mi>c</mi></mrow></math>),
  modeling Bondi-like accretion of rotating, nonrelativistic gas with
  weak vertical magnetic fields onto a rapidly spinning BH, achieving
  inflow equilibrium out to <math
  display="inline"><mrow><mi>r</mi><mo>≳</mo><msup><mrow><mn>10</mn></mrow><mrow><mn>3</mn></mrow></msup><msub><mrow><mi>r</mi></mrow><mrow><mi
  mathvariant="normal">g</mi></mrow></msub></mrow></math>. We find
  that, regardless of scale separation or ambient gas rotation, all
  simulations reach a magnetically arrested disk (MAD) state in which
  the BH becomes magnetically saturated. In this state, the mass
  inflow rate follows a universal radial scaling relative to the Bondi
  rate: <math display="inline"><msub><mover
  accent="true"><mi>M</mi><mo>˙</mo></mover><mrow><mi>in</mi></mrow></msub><mo
  stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>r</mi><mo
  stretchy="false">)</mo><mo>/</mo><msub><mover
  accent="true"><mi>M</mi><mo>˙</mo></mover><mi
  mathvariant="normal">B</mi></msub><mo>∼</mo><mo
  stretchy="false">(</mo><mi>r</mi><mo>/</mo><msub><mi>r</mi><mi
  mathvariant="normal">B</mi></msub><msup><mo
  stretchy="false">)</mo><mi>s</mi></msup></math> with <math
  display="inline"><mi>s</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>0.66</mn><mo>±</mo><mn>0.03</mn></math>.
  The MAD state self-regulates through jets, outflows, and magnetic
  flux eruptions that can ultimately disrupt coherent angular momentum
  inflow, giving rise to a rocking accretion disk (RAD) state. This
  RAD state features chaotically oriented inflows, weak intermittent
  jets, and a steeper inflow slope of <math
  display="inline"><mi>s</mi><mo>=</mo><mn>0.87</mn><mo>±</mo><mn>0.05</mn></math>,
  along with significantly weaker outflows. For rapidly spinning BHs,
  the MAD and RAD BH accretion rates become comparable at typical
  scale separations, <math display="inline"><msub><mi>r</mi><mi
  mathvariant="normal">B</mi></msub><mo>/</mo><msub><mi>r</mi><mi
  mathvariant="normal">g</mi></msub><mo>≳</mo><msup><mn>10</mn><mn>5</mn></msup></math>.
  The weaker outflows in the RAD state allow large-scale inflows to
  resume, eventually restoring the MAD state and enabling a repeating
  MAD-RAD cycle. We find that the MAD-RAD timescales can last from a
  few to hundreds of Bondi timescales, <math
  display="inline"><msub><mi>t</mi><mi
  mathvariant="normal">B</mi></msub><mo>∼</mo><mn>0.2</mn><mtext> </mtext><mtext> </mtext><mi>Myr</mi><mo>×</mo><mo
  stretchy="false">(</mo><msub><mi>r</mi><mi
  mathvariant="normal">B</mi></msub><mo>/</mo><msup><mn>10</mn><mn>5</mn></msup><msub><mi>r</mi><mi
  mathvariant="normal">g</mi></msub><msup><mo
  stretchy="false">)</mo><mrow><mn>3</mn><mo>/</mo><mn>2</mn></mrow></msup><mo>×</mo><mo
  stretchy="false">(</mo><msub><mi>M</mi><mrow><mi>BH</mi></mrow></msub><mo>/</mo><msup><mn>10</mn><mn>9</mn></msup><msub><mi>M</mi><mo
  stretchy="false">⊙</mo></msub><mo stretchy="false">)</mo></math>,
  where <math
  display="inline"><msub><mi>M</mi><mrow><mi>BH</mi></mrow></msub></math>
  is the BH mass, potentially setting the duty cycle of jetted active
  galactic nucleus outbursts, like M87*.
