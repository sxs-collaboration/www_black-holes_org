abstract: |
  The gravitational-wave signal GW250114 was observed by the two LIGO
  detectors with a network matched-filter signal-to-noise ratio of 80.
  The signal was emitted by the coalescence of two black holes with
  near-equal masses <math

  stretchy="false">⊙</mo></mrow></msub></mrow></math> and <math
  display="inline"><mrow><msub><mrow><mi>m</mi></mrow><mrow><mn>2</mn></mrow></msub><mo>=</mo><msubsup><mrow><mn>32.2</mn></mrow><mrow><mo>-</mo><mn>1.3</mn></mrow><mrow><mo>+</mo><mn>0.8</mn></mrow></msubsup><msub><mrow><mi>M</mi></mrow><mrow><mo
  stretchy="false">⊙</mo></mrow></msub></mrow></math>, and small spins
  <math

  (90% credibility) and negligible eccentricity <math
  display="inline"><mi>e</mi><mrow><mo>≤</mo><mn>0.03</mn></mrow></math>.
  Postmerger data excluding the peak region are consistent with the
  dominant quadrupolar <math display="inline"><mrow><mo
  stretchy="false">(</mo><mo>ℓ</mo><mo>=</mo><mo
  stretchy="false">|</mo><mi>m</mi><mo
  stretchy="false">|</mo><mo>=</mo><mn>2</mn><mo
  stretchy="false">)</mo></mrow></math> mode of a Kerr black hole and
  its first overtone. We constrain the modes’ frequencies to <math
  display="inline"><mo>±</mo><mrow><mn>30</mn><mo>%</mo></mrow></math>
  of the Kerr spectrum, providing a test of the remnant’s Kerr nature.
  We also examine Hawking’s area law, also known as the second law of
  black hole mechanics, which states that the total area of the black
  hole event horizons cannot decrease with time. A range of analyses
  that exclude up to five of the strongest merger cycles confirm that
  the remnant area is larger than the sum of the initial areas to high
  credibility.
