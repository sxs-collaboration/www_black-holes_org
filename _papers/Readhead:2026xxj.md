abstract: |
  We present evidence of 2:1 harmonic periodicities in two
  supermassive black hole binary (SMBHB) candidates (PKS J0805-0111
  and PKS 2131-021) in the Owens Valley Radio Observatory (OVRO) +
  University of Michigan Radio Astronomical Observatory (UMRAO)
  monitoring programs. This potential discovery follows the previous
  detection of a 2:1 harmonic periodicity in the SMBHB candidate
  J1309+1154 from these programs. If confirmed, these suggest a
  physical origin of blazars exhibiting strong periodicities favoring
  2:1 commensurabilities in their light curves. There are significant
  differences between the 2:1 harmonics in these three blazars, which
  may be due to differences in their central engines. These three
  SMBHB candidates demonstrate the importance of careful dissection,
  i.e. cutting apart and close examination, of blazar light curves, to
  reveal underlying evidence of coherence between different aspects of
  the periodicities observed in blazars in the presence of correlated
  random noise variations. This is particularly important for blazar
  SMBHB searches, given that the observed periods thus far are all
  longer than 2 years, and can disappear for long timespans. Therefore
  confirming evidence, such as harmonics, may be crucial in confirming
  the reality of periodicities, and identifying blazar SMBHB
  candidates. We present four models that can account for 2:1
  commensurable periodicities in blazar light curves, together with
  explanations for why, on these models, shorter period SMBHB
  candidates have not thus far been observed, but might be
  anticipated, or not, in the future, depending on the model, thereby
  making these models discriminatory. Arguments pro and con the
  reality of these periodicities are presented.
