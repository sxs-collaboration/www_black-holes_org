abstract: |
  The uncertain calibration of gravitational wave detectors is one
  source of systematic error in gravitational wave inference. While
  generally small, this uncertainty can be accounted for by modeling
  and marginalizing over calibration corrections. In this work we
  extend the standard frequency-domain approach to calibration
  marginalization to the time domain using basis splines, in such a
  way that the marginalization is analytic. We apply this method to
  the time-domain analysis of black hole ringdown, in particular the
  two highest signal-to-noise events detected so far, GW250114 and
  GW250207. For GW250114 we continue to find strong evidence for two
  ringdown modes, after marginalizing over the small calibration
  uncertainty at the time of the event. For GW250207, the calibration
  of the LIGO-Hanford detector was uncertain, and calibration
  marginalization is required for a coherent analysis using both LIGO
  detectors. We find that when calibration effects are included the
  remnant mass and spins inferred during ringdown agree with
  inferences from the full signal. Despite the high signal-to-noise of
  GW250207, we find no evidence for the presence of a second mode in
  the ringdown, and as a result deviations from relativity during
  ringdown are not well constrained.
