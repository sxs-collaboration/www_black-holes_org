abstract: |
  The mass and spin distributions of merging binary black holes offer
  clues to their formation pathways, which remain uncertain. In this
  work, we model the population of binary black holes using generic
  subpopulations with distinct mass-ratio, spin-magnitude, and spin-
  tilt distributions, whose relative contributions vary with primary
  mass. Using the 259 binary black holes in the fifth LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA
  catalog (GWTC-5), we resolve three subpopulations with low, medium,
  and high spins. The low-spin population dominates for primary masses
  near \(35 M_\odot\), contributes roughly half of the binaries for
  primary masses of \(10 M_\odot\), and favors nearly equal mass
  ratios overall; its spins are consistent with both black holes
  having negligible spin, with a log Bayes factor of \(\log_{10}B
  =6.1^{+0.2}_{-0.3}\) in favor of vanishing spins. The medium-spin
  population accounts for the other half of the population around
  primary masses of \(10 M_\odot\) and favors spin magnitudes \(χ_i
  \approx 0.2\) for both components, preferentially aligned
  orientations, and comparable mass ratios. The high-spin population
  dominates above primary masses of \(60 M_\odot\) and also
  contributes at lower masses \(\sim 20 M_\odot\); it favors a rapidly
  spinning primary (\(χ_1 \approx 0.7\)), a broad range of secondary
  spins, widely distributed spin orientations, and a broad mass-ratio
  distribution. Allowing for a fourth subpopulation yields no distinct
  additional mass-ratio or spin distribution, as it becomes degenerate
  with existing features. To resolve highly concentrated features in
  spin, we control the variance of standard Monte Carlo likelihood
  estimators using truncated Gaussian mixture models. Taken together,
  our results add to a growing body of evidence for substructure in
  the population of LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA black holes, providing further
  clues to uncover their formation histories.
