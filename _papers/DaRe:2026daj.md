abstract: |
  Gravitational-wave science requires waveform templates to be
  computed at future null infinity, the asymptotic boundary of
  spacetime. Numerical solutions of Einstein's equations typically
  rely on a Cauchy evolution on a finite computational domain, but can
  produce waveforms at infinity by performing a subsequent
  characteristic evolution that compactifies the spacetime. Such
  waveforms capture expected physical effects, like gravitational-wave
  memory and late-time, power-law tails. But this characteristic
  evolution requires initial data consistent with the interior Cauchy
  solution to avoid producing spurious, long-lived transients. A
  careful analysis shows that previous prescriptions for this initial
  data were not sufficiently consistent and provides a way to remedy
  the problem. The new initial data yield accurate waveforms from the
  first cycle onward. The construction is independent of the Cauchy
  formulation and enables the production of catalogs of robust
  waveforms at infinity.
