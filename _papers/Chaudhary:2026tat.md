abstract: |
  The improved sensitivity of upcoming gravitational-wave detectors
  will require correspondingly more accurate binary black hole (BBH)
  simulations. Given the high cost of such simulations, it is
  essential to understand their sources of error and use computational
  resources efficiently. We study sources of error in BBH simulations
  performed with the Spectral Einstein Code (SpEC) and seek to
  understand what limits their accuracy. In particular, we focus on
  the inspiral phase because it is the longest-running part of the
  simulation and accounts for the majority of the waveform error. We
  find that targeted changes to simulation parameters and resolution
  in different parts of the computational domain reduce constraint
  violations and gravitational waveform differences. We also discuss
  more fundamental limitations on our accuracy that become important
  at higher resolutions: floating-point errors and large differences
  in the scales of the fundamental variables. Addressing these
  limitations will require substantial changes to both the SpEC code
  and the formulation it uses to evolve Einstein's equations. Many of
  the improvements and changes discussed in this paper are likely to
  be relevant to other numerical relativity codes.
