##### Research Projects

Mergers of black holes and neutron stars in binaries; Neutrino physics and nucleosynthesis; Radiation transport Long term evolution of accretion disks

##### Biography

I am currently an Associate Professor at the University of New Hampshire, where I moved in 2017.
Before that, I was Einstein post-doctoral fellow at Lawrence Berkeley National Lab (LBL), and a
postdoctoral fellow at CITA. My research involves
studying mergers of compact objects (black holes and neutron stars) through numerical simulations in
general relativity, as well as the long term evolution of accretion disks. I focus particularly on
nuclear astrophysics in neutron star mergers, including neutrino transport, neutrino-matter
interactions, r-process nucleosynthesis, and studies of the dense matter equation of state.

I obtained engineering degrees from both the Free University of Brussels (ULB) and the Ecole
Centrale Paris (ECP) in 2005, before moving to the United States for my PhD. I graduated from
Cornell in 2011.
